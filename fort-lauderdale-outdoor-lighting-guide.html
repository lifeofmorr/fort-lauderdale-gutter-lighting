Homeowner Guide · Outdoor Lighting

Outdoor lighting in Broward, explained plainly.

Three different products all get called "outdoor lighting": permanent roofline systems, landscape lighting, and holiday installation. Here's what each one actually is, what it typically costs in this market, what moves the price up or down, and what to sort out with your HOA before anyone climbs a ladder.

The three kinds

Three products, three different jobs

The ranges below are the published ranges from my dedicated Fort Lauderdale outdoor lighting site — the same numbers quoted there, so you can check me. Your exact number always comes from a free consult and a measured design, in writing.

Permanent roofline lighting

App-controlled LEDs in a discreet, color-matched track under your eaves. Warm white on ordinary nights, team colors on Sundays, red-and-green in December — every holiday, every year, with no ladder and no seasonal install bill. Typical single-family home: $2,000–$6,000.

Landscape lighting

A designed low-voltage system for palms, paths, pools, and architecture — the biggest single curb-appeal upgrade a South Florida home can make, and a genuine security upgrade on top of it. Average project: about $4,000. Smaller starter designs begin lower; waterfront and estate properties run higher.

The concierge version of Christmas lights: design, installation, in-season service, takedown, and storage, all handled. You point at the house; it lights up. Typical home: $1,500–$4,500 per season. Calendars fill by late November — this is the fast-moving one.

What drives cost

Why two neighbors get two very different quotes

Lighting is priced off your property, not off a menu — which is why honest quotes start with a walk-through. The things that actually move the number:

  • Roofline length and shape. Straight runs are simple; every gable, peak, and return adds track, fittings, and time. A long Weston or Parkland estate roofline is a different job from a compact ranch in Coral Springs.
  • Stories and access. Second-story eaves need taller equipment and more care. Safe access is part of the price whether it's itemized or not.
  • Fixture count and palm height (landscape). Every uplight on a royal palm, every path light along a long drive, adds fixtures and wire. Tall palms cost more to light well than short ones — the beam has further to travel.
  • Transformer size and zones. Bigger designs split into zones with separate control. More zones, more hardware, more flexibility.
  • Wire runs across hardscape. Crossing pavers, driveways, and pool decks cleanly takes more labor than running through planting beds.
  • Holiday scope. Roofline only is one price; roofline plus wrapped trees, wreaths, and yard features is another.

What should not move the number: how expensive your house looks. If a quote seems to be priced off your zip code rather than your roofline — and waterfront addresses from Lighthouse Point to Boca Raton see plenty of that — get the measured second quote.

Choosing

Which one is right for your house?

Choose permanent roofline if what you really want is the holidays handled forever — plus a clean warm-white look the other 330 nights. The honest math: it costs more upfront than a single holiday season of professional installation, and it replaces that per-season bill permanently. Over the years most homeowners stay in a house, that's the comparison that matters.

Choose landscape lighting if your property's best features disappear at sunset — mature palms, a pool, architecture worth showing. It's the year-round curb-appeal and security play, and it layers with either of the others.

Choose holiday installation if you want the full display this season without owning anything — new house, hosting year, or testing how much you actually enjoy a lit home before committing to permanent. Plenty of holiday clients convert to permanent the following year with their eyes open.

HOAs and local rules

The Broward HOA reality

A large share of Broward and Palm Beach homes sit in communities with architectural guidelines, and exterior lighting is exactly the kind of thing those guidelines cover. The honest general picture — your own community's documents are the authority, not this page:

  • Approval first. Many communities require an architectural review application for exterior additions, permanent lighting included. Approval before installation is a formality; approval after is a fight. We check your guidelines together before anything is quoted.
  • Discreet wins. Permanent roofline tracks sit under the eave in a channel matched to your trim color, and the lights themselves are small and evenly spaced. It's the most approval-friendly format there is — visibly, the house just glows.
  • Holiday windows and timers. Some communities set display seasons, shut-off times, or brightness expectations for holiday lighting. A system on a timer follows those rules automatically — one more reason the app control matters beyond convenience.
  • Be a good neighbor. Aimed landscape fixtures and warm color temperatures keep light on your property, not in your neighbor's bedroom. Good design handles this; it's worth asking any installer how they handle it.

None of this is a reason to skip lighting — communities in Palm Beach Gardens, Weston, and Parkland are full of beautifully lit homes. It's a reason to sequence the paperwork before the ladder.

Coastal note

Salt air is a spec, not a detail

From Harbor Beach to Hillsboro Beach, homes near the water live in salt spray, and lighting hardware feels it first. Fixtures and track that aren't built for coastal exposure pit, corrode at connections, and fail early — the cheap set from a big-box store is often a two-season purchase here whether it's labeled that way or not.

When you compare quotes, ask what the fixtures and track are made of and how connections are sealed. It's the same lesson as gutter guards, one page over: in South Florida, material is destiny.

Questions

Lighting costs, answered

How much does permanent roofline lighting cost in Fort Lauderdale?

Most single-family homes in Broward run $2,000–$6,000 for a professionally installed permanent roofline system, depending on roofline length, stories, and access. Those are the published ranges on my lighting site; the free consult prices your exact roofline, in writing.

Do I need HOA approval?

Often, yes — many Broward and Palm Beach communities require architectural approval for exterior additions, and it's far easier to get before installation than forgiveness after. Permanent tracks sit under the eave in a color-matched channel, which is why they're the most approval-friendly option. We check your community's guidelines together before anything is quoted.

When should I book holiday installation?

Earlier than feels reasonable. Professional holiday installation calendars in South Florida typically fill by late November, and the design-install-service-takedown packages go first. If you're reading this in the fall, treat it as the season to ask, not the season to start thinking about it.
